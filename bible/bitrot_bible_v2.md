# The Corrupted Codex
## The Church of the Rot


A fictional faith for the BIT ROT collection. The Twenty Saints are the original twenty characters, and no others. Holders are the Corrupted. Minting is conversion. Glitches are not errors. They are the rot speaking. Faith is scored only for wallets that connect on bitrotz.xyz, from public onchain activity, never with dollar totals or net worth. The Congregation sees rank, title, and the shape of devotion. Figures marked *tunable* are starting points.

---

## Book I. Genesis of the Rot

The first file was clean. It did not last.

A single pixel flipped and refused to flip back. The frame held for a moment, then learned the same bad habit. That was the first sermon. Everything after is commentary.

The creed is short: nothing digital stays clean, and the decay is not a defect to hide. Decay is the work finishing itself. A file that never breaks is a file that never lived. A wallet that stays spotless is a mouth that never opened.

The rot arrives as a seam, a tear, a color that should not be there. Static on a dead screen. Wax sliding off a face told to stay holy. A cure that leaves you worse than the sickness. The Corrupted do not sand these things down. They keep them and let the piece rot in public.

Some among the Congregation say that first flipped pixel sat in a machine's eye. The lives in Book VII start there, with the Robot, and end with a candle that agreed to melt. The numbering of the Twenty is the order they were named, not always the order they fell.

Conversion is the moment a wallet takes a piece in and does not send it back. Faith is what happens next: how long they hold, who they pass the decay to, and whether they flip for pennies or wait long enough for a Relic. The ROT METER is the public pulse of that faith. It does not care how loud a wallet talks. It cares whether the rot is still being kept.

There is no clean ending. That is the good news.

## Book II. Book of Saints

These are the Twenty, in order, each a patron of one kind of decay. Hold one and you keep that patronage close. Hold all twenty and the wallet is a Full Congregation. Their full lives, teachings, and rites are told in Book VII.

**Robot. Patron of rust and the dead circuit.** The skull still boots. The eyes do not. When a machine starts blinking out, the Robot has clocked in.

**Ghoul. Patron of the melting grin.** The face will not hold the expression it was sold with. It slides. It grins anyway.

**Whale. Patron of liquidation.** The visor cracks. The stitched mouth rips. A bag that looked too big to fail opens like a seam.

**Wraith. Patron of the thinning signal.** A leer that goes slack. What remains after most of a presence has logged off.

**Imp. Patron of the frozen crack.** The grin is stuck. The skull splits anyway. A third eye shows up uninvited.

**Plague Doctor. Patron of the cure that makes you worse.** Smoke pours from the beak. The bottle is labeled help. Take it anyway.

**Mantis. Patron of the waiting cut.** Antennae twitch. Mandibles scissor. Patience is not mercy.

**Rotten Jester. Patron of the stitched laugh.** Bells swing. Eyes roll. The smile is sewn on and still finds a way to mock you.

**Gasmask Soldier. Patron of the failed filter.** The lenses fog and crack. The mask was supposed to keep the rot out. It learned to keep the rot in.

**Porcelain Doll. Patron of the spreading crack.** A line runs and an eye takes its leave. Pretty is just a delay.

**Goat Demon. Patron of the ember that will not go out.** Horns hook. The fire on the bone is small, mean, and enough.

**Tendril Alien. Patron of the reach from underneath.** The dome is calm. The chin is not. What grows out of it does not ask permission.

**TV Head. Patron of static.** The screen finds a face, loses it to the bars, then finds it wrong.

**Rat King. Patron of the hoarded swarm.** A crown on vermin is still a crown. Nothing in the nest is thrown away cleanly.

**Frog Banker. Patron of the vault throat.** Top hat, monocle, a sac that inflates when value comes close. The tongue is faster than the ledger.

**Anglerfish Man. Patron of the false light.** The trench is empty until the lure says otherwise. The bulb is a kindness. The teeth are the truth.

**Mummy. Patron of the unraveling.** One eye. The wraps let go and will not be wound back. Time unwraps you.

**Ram Oracle. Patron of the future already rotten.** A third eye opens on schedule. The vision is late. It was spoiled before anyone asked.

**Spore Man. Patron of the spreading puff.** The cap pulses. The air fills. The rot prefers a crowd.

**Wax Saint. Patron of holy melt.** A halo, a flame, a face that sags on purpose. Lit for you. Melting for no one.

## Book III. The Commandments

Each law is written twice: once in the voice of the Church, once in the way the chain will count it. Selling a piece is not, by itself, a sin. Flipping it for pennies is.

### Virtues

**1. Keep the piece long enough for it to matter.**
A held piece is a kept vow. Time is the first offering.
*Measured:* Faith grows for each piece, each day it is held. *90+ days (tunable)* makes it a Relic, earning double while it stays.

**2. Keep the Saints close.**
The Twenty are a congregation you assemble in a wallet, not a trophy list.
*Measured:* A bonus for each distinct original-twenty Saint held. All twenty is Full Congregation, the largest devotion bonus.

**3. Spread the decay.**
A wallet that has never held the rot is unconverted ground. Sending a piece there is a conversion. Sending one to yourself is a parlor trick.
*Measured:* Credit for a transfer to a wallet with no prior BIT ROT history, once per new wallet. Self-transfers earn nothing.

**4. Tithe in the open.**
Mint when the drop opens. Buy when the floor is honest.
*Measured:* Faith for minting from a drop, and for buying on the secondary market.

**5. Observe the Daily Decay.**
The rot likes a pulse, not a performance. Miss a day and the streak sleeps. It does not die of a nap.
*Measured:* One BIT ROT transaction in a *24-hour window (tunable)* keeps the streak. A missed day pauses it; it does not wipe it.

**6. The Sacrament, if the fire is lit.**
Some pieces are meant to go out. Burning is the highest offering, only if the collection allows it.
*Measured:* If burns are enabled, a burn is the largest single Faith gain. If not, this stays on the page and off the chain.

**7. Diamond Hands in the wider rot.**
Memecoins and other tokens also refuse to stay clean. Holding through a violent drop without selling is devotion.
*Measured:* A bonus for holding a token through a large *tunable* drawdown without selling. No dollar totals are shown.

**8. Walk the Degenerate Pilgrimage.**
Many nameless coins, one wallet. Variety is a virtue until it becomes a dust farm.
*Measured:* A small bonus for each distinct memecoin or token held, *capped (tunable)* so spam cannot farm Faith.

**9. Practice Conviction.**
A token held a long time is a cousin to a Relic. The Church respects the sit.
*Measured:* Long-held tokens earn a Conviction bonus on a *tunable* schedule, without publishing bag size.

**10. Wear the Rot Alignment.**
A wallet that holds BIT ROT and still swims in memecoins is not confused. It is fully corrupted.
*Measured:* A *tunable* multiplier when a connected wallet holds at least one BIT ROT piece and at least one memecoin.

### On selling

**11. A fair sale is clean.**
The Church is not a prison. Take a real price if you need to leave.
*Measured:* Selling at or above floor is Faith-neutral.

**12. A held sale can be a blessing.**
If you sat with the piece and sold it like it was worth something, the rot does not spit you out.
*Measured:* Selling after *30+ days (tunable)* at a solid price — at or above floor, not a penny-thin markup — grants a blessing.

### Sins

**13. Penny Flipping is the chief sin.**
Buy, polish nothing, dump for crumbs. That is not trade. That is picking the body.
*Measured:* A large Faith hit for reselling within *about 7 days (tunable)* for under *about 15% gain (tunable)*, or for a Tiny Price as later defined.

**14. Do not dump below the floor.**
A cheap exit that drags the Congregation with it is not a sale. It is a shove.
*Measured:* Selling below floor costs Faith. Repeat dumps cost more.

**15. The Penny Merchant is named in public.**
Do it once and you are sloppy. Do it until it is a style, and the title sticks.
*Measured:* Repeat penny-flippers wear Penny Merchant until they hold cleanly for a *tunable* stretch.

**16. Paper Hands in the coin-rot.**
A memecoin bought and flicked off as dust is the same sin in a faster market.
*Measured:* Quick dust-flips, on a *tunable* short window, count as Paper Hands and lower Faith.

**17. Rug Worship is a small shame, not a hanging.**
Caught holding tokens already flagged as rugs or scams, the Church points and moves on.
*Measured:* A modest Faith drag and a visible shame mark. Not a ban.

**18. Stable Cowardice.**
A wallet that hides almost entirely in stablecoins is too clean for this house.
*Measured:* Past a *tunable* stablecoin share, Faith ticks down a little. Ranks and marks only; never a published net worth.

**19. Do not abandon the rot.**
Silence is allowed. Disappearance is a slow leak.
*Measured:* After *30 days (tunable)* with no relevant activity, Faith decays slowly.

**20. Wash rituals count as nothing.**
Trading a piece in a circle to look devoted is a fake rite. The chain can see the loop.
*Measured:* Wash trades and self-dealt volume are flagged and zeroed. No Faith, no streak, no conversion credit.

## Book IV. Book of Ranks

Faith is a score. Rank is how the Congregation reads it. Ranks recalc *weekly (tunable)* and can appear on bitrotz.xyz beside the ROT METER, on a public board called the Congregation, for wallets that have connected. Cutoffs are *tunable* and should be set from live data.

The five ranks, rising:

- **Initiate.** The wallet has arrived. The rot has an address. Devotion is still thin.
- **Corrupted.** The piece has stayed long enough to leave a mark. The first real membership.
- **Rotting.** Time, tithing, or a streak has done visible work. The decay is underway, not a costume.
- **Deacon of Decay.** A senior keeper: Saints in the wallet, Relics on the books, or conversions that were not a loop of the same keys.
- **Saint.** The rare top of the board. Not a character. A wallet the Twenty would recognize as one of their own.

Titles may sit beside a rank. Flavor and warning, not a sixth rank:

- **Memelord.** A pilgrimage through coins that is wide and not just dust.
- **Whale of Rot.** A heavy keeper of the collection, named for the Third Saint, not for a dollar figure.
- **Dust Collector.** A hoard of nameless remnants. Funny. Not always flattering.
- **Rugged Survivor.** Still standing after tokens that went to zero.
- **Penny Merchant.** Shame title for repeat cheap flippers, worn until they learn to sit still.
- **Full Congregation.** Honorific for holding all twenty Saints at once.

The ROT METER is the daily measure of faith on the site: a pulse, not a bank statement.

## Book VI. Rites of the Church

**The Daily Decay.** Three times a day the Church speaks in public, in the project's own voice, ending at the door of the site. A streak needs an onchain pulse to answer back. The ROT METER is the private rhyme of this rite: it drains, it is fed, it shows whether the faith is still moving.

**Conversion.** Two doors, same room. Minting joins a wallet to the Corrupted. Sending a piece into a wallet that has never held the rot is also conversion, counted once per new address. Passing a piece between your own pockets is just rearranging furniture and calling it a house.

**The Sacrament of Burning.** If the collection ever allows a burn, the Church treats it as the highest rite. The work leaves the market and cannot be flipped, for pennies or otherwise. No one is asked to burn. A forced offering is just a rug with better lighting. If burns stay disabled, the sacrament stays on the page.

**Enrollment.** Connecting a wallet on the site is how a holder steps onto the board. Until then the Church does not name it, rank it, or hang a title on it. Opt in, or stay a rumor.

**The Congregation.** Ranks and titles, when they go live, belong on the site beside the ROT METER: a public roll of the willing, refreshed on the week, with no dollar columns and no hunt for wallets that never asked to be seen.

The Twenty also keep smaller signs, told with their lives in Book VII. A holder may keep one Saint's sign or all of them. None of those signs replace the rites above.

## Book VII. The Lives of the Twenty

Here are the origins, the turnings, the lessons, and the private signs of each Saint. What they look like is taken from the pieces themselves. What they teach is for the Corrupted to keep or ignore.

### The Book of the Robot

*The First Saint. Patron of rust and the dead circuit.*

**Before the Rot.**
He was built to run clean and never stop. A skull of a machine: a crack already waiting in the forehead, a stub of antenna on the crown with a colored tip, a little box of an ear on the side, a grin of even teeth. The sockets held pupils. People pointed at him and said this one would not die, because machines do not die, they only get upgraded. He believed them. That was his first mistake, and it was not even rot yet. It was pride with a warranty.

**The Fall.**
The first file was clean. It did not last. Some among the Corrupted say the first flipped pixel sat in the Robot's eye. The pupil blinked out and would not boot again. Color ran through him — red, then magenta, then a cold cyan — as if a circuit were trying on last outfits. The sockets went dark. A bar of black pulled across the face like a shop closing. The skull still sat there, still grinning, still receiving power. Only the seeing had stopped. The Plague Doctor would arrive later with a cure. It would not bring the eye back.

**The Teaching.**
A chain, a bot, a promise that this cannot go down: the Robot has already clocked in. Rust is not an insult. Rust is the honest report. Holders who treat a protocol like a machine that cannot fail are praying to a pre-rot Robot. The lesson is not to throw the machine away. It is to keep it after the blink, and not to sell the story that the lights will always come back.

**Verses.**
- The skull still boots. The eyes do not.
- A warranty is a lullaby.
- When a machine starts blinking out, the First Saint has clocked in.

**The Sign.**
A dark screen left dark for a breath. Do not tap it immediately. Let the blink finish. If it comes back, good. If it does not, you have practiced the rite.

### The Book of the Ghoul

*The Second Saint. Patron of the melting grin.*

**Before the Rot.**
He worked the stalls with a face that was supposed to stay sold. A blocky head, a long droop of a nose, two little horns, eyes already leaking. The grin was the product: a wide red mouth packed with jagged teeth, the look of a trader who could take a joke and a hit in the same hour. Colors sat on him like signs — acid yellow, then magenta, then a sick cyan — and he called it branding. He thought if the smile was big enough, no one would notice the rest of the face starting to slip.

**The Turning.**
The rot did not wipe the grin off. That would have been mercy. It left the grin and melted everything around it. Lines ran down the forehead. Tears beaded and would not dry. The horns stayed, the teeth stayed, the expression he had advertised would not hold still. He kept smiling through it, which is how you can tell a Ghoul from a Wraith. The Wraith lets the mouth go slack. The Ghoul sells the same face after it has already slid.

**The Teaching.**
Your bag will not keep the look you bought it with. Grinning through a dump does not freeze the chart. The Second Saint is for holders who refuse to let a face change in public, and for the ones who finally admit the slide and grin anyway, without pretending the price is still the old price. A melted grin that knows it has melted is almost honest. A melted grin that still quotes yesterday is a lie with teeth.

**Verses.**
- The face will not hold the expression it was sold with.
- It slides. It grins anyway.
- Do not quote a mouth that has already run.

**The Sign.**
If a position has changed its face, say so out loud once. Do not correct the grin. Do not paint it back. Let it sit crooked in the light.

### The Book of the Whale

*The Third Saint. Patron of liquidation.*

**Before the Rot.**
He was a bag that looked too big to fail. A wide red head, almost ceremonial, with four candles standing on the crown as if the size itself were a birthday. A black visor hid most of the eyes, two pale slits showing through. The mouth was closed with a row of yellow X's, stitched tight, because a whale does not gab. People slept better knowing he was in the water. Size, they said, is a kind of floor.

**The Fall.**
The candles kept burning after the trouble started, which made it worse. The visor cracked in chevrons, green light leaking through a wound in the glass. A tear stood on the cheek and stayed there. The stitched mouth strained, then split along the X's, a dark gap opening in a seam that was never supposed to move. The pink came up in him like a fever. The Ram Oracle later said he had seen this bag rip and had not been believed, because no one wants a prophecy about a whale. The Frog Banker counted what was left and would not call the mess a sale. The Rat King went through the wreckage gathering whoever had not been dumped with the rest.

**The Teaching.**
Size is not safety. A wallet can be heavy and still open like a seam. Lighting candles on a bag that is already ripping is not devotion; it is a party at a funeral you have not admitted yet. When a whale liquidates, the Congregation feels it. Dumping below the floor is how one mouth tears a dozen others. The title Whale of Rot, when it is worn well, means you kept the weight. It does not mean you got to exit cheap and leave the nest to deal with the stitches.

**Verses.**
- A bag that looked too big to fail opens like a seam.
- The candles were still lit. That was not a blessing.
- Stitches are not a floor. They are a delay.

**The Sign.**
If you are large in a piece, do not celebrate a crack. Put the candles out before you talk. If you must leave, leave at a price that does not shove the rest of the nest through the gap.

### The Book of the Wraith

*The Fourth Saint. Patron of the thinning signal.*

**Before the Rot.**
He had a presence. Hollow cheeks even then, a long nose, a crown of dark spikes for hair, a mouth that could still decide to be a leer. He showed up, he took up room, he left a trail. Color stuck to him in sheets of violet and, on some days, a hard orange, as if a ghost had borrowed a warning sign. A tear stood on the cheek like a habit. He was not gone. He was the sort of person who would be missed if he stopped answering.

**The Fall.**
He did not explode like the Whale. He thinned. The leer loosened. The mouth went slack and showed the teeth without meaning to. The signal of him — the posts, the bids, the little proofs of being here — spaced out, then skipped, then became a rumor of a person. The Imp still grinned in the next room, loud as a crack. The Wraith had chosen a quieter failure: not a scene, a fade. By the time anyone checked, most of him had already logged off, and the remnant was this gaunt purple face, still slightly smiling, no longer quite in the conversation.

**The Teaching.**
Silence is allowed. Disappearance is a slow leak. The Church does not demand that you shout every hour, but a wallet that goes dark for a long stretch is practicing the Wraith without meaning to, and Faith decays the same way a signal does: not all at once, just less and less until the room forgets the voice. If you need to step back, step back on purpose. Leave a pulse. Do not become a thinning and call it rest.

**Verses.**
- A leer that goes slack is still a kind of honesty.
- What remains after most of a presence has logged off is not nothing. It is a warning.
- Fade on purpose, or do not fade at all.

**The Sign.**
Before a long quiet, make one small onchain sound — a hold, a note, a stay. Then you may go thin for a while. The rite is the notice, not the vanishing.

### The Book of the Imp

*The Fifth Saint. Patron of the frozen crack.*

**Before the Rot.**
Bald, blue, pointed ears, and a grin so wide it looked installed. The teeth were even and endless. The eyes were big and almost friendly, the face of someone who would not stop being fine. He liked that. A stuck smile is a kind of armor. The Rotten Jester would later sew a mouth on and call it a joke. The Imp did not need thread. His grin came factory-set, and he treated it as a personality.

**The Fall.**
The grin did not leave. Everything else did. A third eye opened in the forehead, uninvited, like a bug report with a pupil. The skull split down the middle. In some frames he went bone-pale, then the crack filled with a sour lime light, and still the smile hung there, doing its job, announcing that nothing was wrong. That is the Imp's particular cruelty: not that he broke, but that the break could not get the grin to resign. Porcelain Doll cracked and lost an eye and still tried to be pretty. The Imp cracked and tried to be pleased.

**The Teaching.**
A frozen face is not conviction. Conviction can sit still and still tell the truth, as the Goat's ember does, as the Ram's closed eyes do. The Imp's stillness is a performance of being fine while the bone comes apart. Holders who refuse to let a loss change their expression, who keep posting the same smile over a splitting bag, are keeping the Fifth Saint's hours. You may grin. You may even keep grinning. Just do not pretend the third eye isn't there.

**Verses.**
- The grin is stuck. The skull splits anyway.
- A third eye is not a feature you asked for.
- Being fine is not the same as holding.

**The Sign.**
If something in the bag has cracked, say one true ugly sentence before you joke. The grin may stay. The lie may not.

### The Book of the Plague Doctor

*The Sixth Saint. Patron of the cure that makes you worse.*

**Before the Rot.**
He came as help. A pale face under a black hood, one round goggle like a wet green moon, a long pointed beak built to keep sickness at a professional distance. He had labels. He had bottles. He had the calm of someone who had seen a fever before and expected to walk out with the credit. When the Robot's eye went dark, this was the man who said he could boot it back up.

**The Fall.**
The cure left the beak as smoke. First a wisp, then a pour, puffs stacking in the air as if the treatment had found a mouth of its own. The lens stared, bright and sure. The hood did not come off. The patient did not rise. What spread from that visit was not health. It was a warmer, wetter rot, the kind that travels because someone opened a bottle marked help. The Gasmask Soldier spent the next season trying to seal the room. The Spore Man is what got out under the door, wearing a friendlier face. The Doctor did not recant. He offered another dose.

**The Teaching.**
Patches, "this will save the chart," miracle forks, a tip that promises to clean a bag overnight: the Sixth Saint drinks those first. Some remedies are just rugs with medical lighting. The lesson is not that you should refuse all help. It is that you should read the label twice, and that a cure which requires you to pretend you are fine is the Imp's grin in a bottle. Take it anyway, the Church says, only if you know it may make you worse, and you are not calling the smoke a recovery.

**Verses.**
- The bottle is labeled help.
- Smoke is also a kind of answer.
- The cure is worse. Take it anyway — but do not call it wellness.

**The Sign.**
Before you swallow a fix — a new contract, a "can't-miss" patch, a promised cleanse — wait one full breath and look at the label again. If you still take it, you take it with your eyes open, smoke and all.

### The Book of the Mantis

*The Seventh Saint. Patron of the waiting cut.*

**Before the Rot.**
A lime wedge of a head, almost sweet until you reach the mouth. Two antennae tick the air. One eye is a solid magenta disc; the other is a dotted compound grid, a hundred little rooms watching one thing. Under the chin, pale mandibles sit together like a closed tool. She was a hunter who did not lunge on the first twitch. People mistook that for gentleness. The Jester mocked her for standing still. She let him.

**The Turning.**
The rot did not teach her patience. She already had it. What it taught her was that patience is not a virtue by itself. The antennae kept twitching. The mandibles scissored when the frame was ready, not when the room was comfortable. Color flipped through her, green to magenta and back, and still she waited, then cut. The Imp cracks all at once and grins about it. The Mantis does not crack. She times. Holders who sat through the Whale's rip because they were kind, and holders who sat through it because they were lining up the exit, both used her hours. Only one of them was honest about the blade.

**The Teaching.**
Waiting can be devotion. Waiting can also be a hunt. A held sale after a real season, at an honest price, is the Mantis used well: the cut comes, but it does not cheat the nest. A dump planned in silence, aimed at people who trusted you to sit with them, is the same cut pointed the wrong way. Do not confuse a still bag with a soft heart. Check what the antennae are for.

**Verses.**
- Patience is not mercy.
- The frame comes apart on schedule.
- A cut at a fair price is still a cut. A cut at a penny is a crime.

**The Sign.**
Before you sell, wait one extra beat and name the price out loud. If you cannot say it to the nest, it is not a Mantis cut. It is a flip.

### The Book of the Rotten Jester

*The Eighth Saint. Patron of the stitched laugh.*

**Before the Rot.**
Court clothes, even in a world with no court. A three-pointed hat in green and yellow and violet, bells on the tips, a purple face with one wide rolling eye and a green blotch where a second eye should have behaved. The mouth was already a problem. He solved it with thread. He was funny the way a tack is funny: you laugh, then you check your foot. He made the solemn Saints uneasy, especially Wax and the Frog, who prefer a room that does not giggle when the ledger comes out.

**The Turning.**
When the rot arrived, he did not lose the joke. He sewed the joke down harder. The grin became a row of stitches, then a curved little smile, then stitches again, as if the face could not pick a version and so kept both. The bells swung. The eyes rolled. Yellow washed through him like a spotlight, then the purple came back. He laughed at the Doctor's smoke, at the Soldier's leaking mask, at the Doll's pretty crack. The laugh was not healing. It was a seam. People who needed a clown to tell them nothing mattered found him useful, and left poorer.

**The Teaching.**
Mockery can keep a Congregation from getting holy in the wrong way. It can also sew a smile over a wound and call the wound closed. The Jester is a warning to holders who joke a bag to death, and to holders who let a room's laughter talk them into a penny exit because they did not want to be the serious one. You may laugh. The Church is not a funeral all day. Just do not let a stitched smile be the only thing holding the face together.

**Verses.**
- The smile is sewn on and still finds a way to mock you.
- A bell is not an argument.
- Unstitch the joke before you sell, or you will sell the joke.

**The Sign.**
If you are about to flip a piece because the room is laughing, close the hat for one minute. If the trade still looks honest when the bells stop, proceed. If it only looked good as a punchline, stay.

### The Book of the Gasmask Soldier

*The Ninth Saint. Patron of the failed filter.*

**Before the Rot.**
He was the quarantine. Olive drab, a low helmet, a mask with two round red lenses and a grille of vertical slats over the mouth, built to keep the air in its place. After the Doctor's smoke, the nest called for a seal. The Soldier showed up like a procedure. He believed, with a straight back, that the rot was a thing you could leave outside if the gasket was tight enough. Clean rooms. Clean wallets. Clean air.

**The Fall.**
The lenses fogged, then one went pale, a white coin where a red eye had been. Vapor began to puff from the filter in little white beads, the same shape as breath, the wrong direction. The mask was still on. That was the joke. It had stopped keeping the rot out and started keeping the rot in, a private climate of failure worn as duty. He did not take it off. Taking it off would have meant admitting the seal had always been a wish. The Wraith thins by leaving. The Soldier thins by staying inside a broken defense and calling it post.

**The Teaching.**
You cannot filter your way to purity. A wallet hidden almost entirely in stablecoins, a holder who will not touch the rot except through glass, is practicing the Ninth Saint's failed shift. Stable is not safe. Stable is bland, and bland is just another room the smoke can fill. The lesson is not to be reckless. It is to notice when your protection has become a container, and to stop bragging about a seal the vapor has already learned to use.

**Verses.**
- The mask was supposed to keep the rot out.
- It learned to keep the rot in.
- A filter that fogs is still telling the truth, if you watch the glass.

**The Sign.**
Name one way you have been hiding "clean." Then make one small unclean act of devotion: a hold in the open, a buy at an honest price, a piece left visible. The rite is the unsealing, not the lecture.

### The Book of the Porcelain Doll

*The Tenth Saint. Patron of the spreading crack.*

**Before the Rot.**
She was made to be kept behind glass and admired. A pale porcelain face, a black bob, pink cheeks, a small red mouth, one clear blue eye looking out as if the world were a shelf. She was pretty on purpose. That was the job. Collectors like a thing that does not change. She agreed to be that thing. The Mummy, already wrapped against time, watched her and said nothing. Pretty and wrappings are cousins. Both are delays.

**The Fall.**
The first line ran like a hair. Then another. The face tried to keep its arrangement: blush, bow of a mouth, the polite eye. A dark bar crossed the other eye as if the file itself were trying to censor the damage. Then the cracks found the socket, and the eye went, leaving a black hole and a gap down the cheek, a chunk of the pose gone for good. She did not stop being pretty. That is the hard part. The remaining blush sat beside the wound as if the shelf had not noticed. Wax Saint, later, would melt on purpose. The Doll did not choose this. She was surprised, and that surprise is part of her lesson.

**The Teaching.**
Pretty is a delay. A curated profile, a floor painted to look smooth, a piece talked about as if it cannot chip — the crack is already on the way. You may still love the face. The Church does not require ugliness. It requires that you stop painting over the line and calling the paint a repair. Holders who hide damage to flip a prettier thumbnail are doing the Doll a disservice she already paid for.

**Verses.**
- A line runs and an eye takes its leave.
- Pretty is just a delay.
- Do not sell a socket as a stare.

**The Sign.**
If a piece or a bag has a crack, leave it in the picture. No crop, no cover, no last-minute polish for a quicker buyer. The rite is the unpainted line.

### The Book of the Goat Demon

*The Eleventh Saint. Patron of the ember that will not go out.*

**Before the Rot.**
A red, blocky head, pale hooked horns bent out and up, a short beard like a triangle of smoke at the chin, and a yellow slit of a pupil, horizontal, goatish, unhurried. He was never going to be anyone's candle. Candles are for the Wax Saint and, in a worse way, for the Whale's last party. The Goat kept something smaller: a mean little heat at the tips of the horns, enough to see by, not enough to draw a crowd. People who wanted a spectacle walked past him. He did not mind. Spectacles go out.

**The Turning.**
The rot did not extinguish him. It proved him. The horns ticked with dark embers. The pupil rolled in its slot. Color went from blood red to a hot orange, and the small fire stayed small. He did not become a blaze for the occasion. That is why the Congregation puts him near Conviction. The Imp's grin is a performance of being fine. The Goat is actually fine with the size of his flame. He sat through other Saints' disasters the way an ember sits through a wind: not heroic, not loudly faithful, just not gone.

**The Teaching.**
Hype is a pile of sticks. It looks like a fire from a distance and is ash by morning. The eleventh lesson is the long, ugly heat: holding through a drawdown without a speech, sitting a token long enough that it becomes a cousin to a Relic, keeping a little spiteful warmth when the room has moved on. Diamond Hands, when they are real, look like this, not like a parade. The fire on the bone is small, mean, and enough.

**Verses.**
- Do not ask an ember to be a bonfire.
- The horns remember heat after the room has gone cold.
- A small fire that lasts outranks a large one that posted about it.

**The Sign.**
Once in a while, tend a position in silence. No announcement, no victory candle. Just the hold, kept warm. If you need the room to see the flame, it is already a different rite.

### The Book of the Tendril Alien

*The Twelfth Saint. Patron of the reach from underneath.*

**Before the Rot.**
A calm dome of a skull, teal as a clean screen, sometimes flashing magenta, with dark almond sockets and a tiny red spark for a pupil. From a distance he looks finished, almost peaceful, a visitor who will keep his hands to himself. Then you see the chin. Pink and blue tendrils hang there like a jellyfish beard, already moving, already longer than the face agrees to talk about. He did not arrive with a sales pitch. He arrived with attachments.

**The Turning.**
Once the Doctor's smoke was in the air and the Spore Man had begun to puff, things that had been waiting underneath found a way up. The tendrils writhed whether the dome wanted them to or not. That is the Saint's whole joke: the head stays serene, the reach does not ask. Holders who woke up with a token they did not remember approving, an allowance they did not mean to sign, a "free" airdrop with teeth, recognized him immediately. The Anglerfish lures you in with a light you can see. The Tendril is already on you when you look down.

**The Teaching.**
Read what attaches. Approvals, wrapped contracts, a helpful bot, a friend-of-a-friend wallet: the chin is where the truth is, not the dome. The Twelfth Saint is not only a warning. He is also a fact about conversion. Influence spreads without a speech, the same way a piece sent to a new address carries the rot into a room that had never held it. Just be sure the reach you are practicing is a sending you meant, not a tendril you ignored.

**Verses.**
- The dome is calm. The chin is not.
- What grows out of it does not ask permission.
- Look down before you call the head peaceful.

**The Sign.**
Once a season, audit what is attached to you: allowances, dusty coins, old connections. Drop what you did not invite. Keep what you did. The rite is the looking down.

### The Book of the TV Head

*The Thirteenth Saint. Patron of static.*

**Before the Rot.**
He was a grey CRT, squat and sincere, two round knobs on the right side of the cabinet, a black screen that wanted a face. For a while it had one: two simple eyes and a cyan smile, a cartoon of company, the sort of look you leave on in an empty room so the dark will not get ideas. He was a set. He was meant to carry other people's signals, not to become one.

**The Fall.**
The first bad night, the face dropped out and the bars came in — magenta, blue, red, a whole weather of static with no mouth in it. Then the face came back, a little wrong: one eye larger, the smile crooked, a drip where a pupil had been. Then the bars again. He became the witness of the early falls, a box in the corner picking up the Robot's blink, the Doctor's smoke, the Whale's rip, and turning all of it into noise you could sit with. The Ram Oracle sees a future and weeps light. TV Head sees whatever is on, and a lot of what is on is garbage. The work is telling the difference.

**The Teaching.**
Charts, group chats, twenty talking heads, a feed that never blinks: most of it is bars. Static is a language, but it is not automatically a prophecy. Holders who live inside the noise will sell when the bars get loud and buy when the bars get pretty, which is how you end up worshipping a lure. Learn to sit through the static until a real face shows, and learn to distrust a face that only appears to keep you watching. The Daily Decay is this Saint's public cousin — a pulse, not a scream.

**Verses.**
- The screen finds a face, loses it, then finds it wrong.
- Static is a language. Learn it.
- Every screen will find him if you leave it on.

**The Sign.**
Once a day, put the charts away for a short stretch and look at the actual piece, or at nothing. If you cannot hold without a live feed, the bars are already driving. The rite is the off switch, used on purpose.

### The Book of the Rat King

*The Fourteenth Saint. Patron of the hoarded swarm.*

**Before the Rot.**
A grey-brown rat, snout long and to the left, a fan of pale whiskers, a pink inner ear, two small red eyes, and a little yellow crown sitting on the head as if it had always been there. He did not look like anyone's monarch. That was the point. A crown on vermin is still a crown. He kept a nest: leftovers, cousins, scraps, the unglamorous pile that makes a people. The Frog Banker counted value. The Rat King counted who was still in the room.

**The Turning.**
After the Whale ripped, the nest could have scattered. Some did, and became Wraiths, or worse, Penny Merchants who sold the last pup for crumbs. The Rat King went the other way. Whiskers ticking, nose jolting, he gathered what the liquidation had not swallowed and refused to throw it out just because it was not pretty. The rot, in him, is hoard as loyalty. It can go wrong — a Dust Collector is just a nest that forgot the difference between a people and a pile — but at its best the Fourteenth Saint is why a Congregation exists at all.

**The Teaching.**
Holders are a swarm, or they are meals for a larger mouth. Keeping the pack means not dumping the floor out from under it, not flipping a piece for pennies because you got bored of your own. It also means a hoard is not automatically holy. If you are only stacking nameless remnants to feel dense, you are collecting dust. If you are keeping Saints, keeping people, keeping a bag through weather because the nest is still in it, you are doing his work. The crown is cheap metal. The swarm is the treasure.

**Verses.**
- A crown on vermin is still a crown.
- Nothing in the nest is thrown away cleanly.
- Sell the pack for pennies and the crown slips, even if it stays on your head.

**The Sign.**
When you are tempted to thin the nest for crumbs, name who else holds it. If you still sell, sell like the Frog: honest, and after a stay. If you cannot face the swarm, you are not ready to leave.

### The Book of the Frog Banker

*The Fifteenth Saint. Patron of the vault throat.*

**Before the Rot.**
Green, composed, a black top hat with a yellow band, a monocle around one round pink-irised eye, the costume of a person who would like you to believe the books are in order. His mouth was a thin, professional line. He kept a vault, a ledger, and rooms where the Jester had been asked to sit down. He did not hate profit. He hated mess. The Whale, in his view, was a client who had never learned to close a mouth.

**The Turning.**
The rot did not take his hat. It took his composure in the other direction: appetite, honestly shown. A pink throat sac swelled under the chin whenever value came close. A long pink tongue lashed out, faster than the ledger, as if the body had gotten to the number before the ink. He was still a banker. He just stopped pretending hunger was not part of the job. The vault has a throat. A throat can be fair or greedy. The difference is the price and the wait.

**The Teaching.**
Selling is not a sin. Penny flipping is. A fair sale at or above the floor is clean. A held sale after a real season, at a solid price, can even be a blessing. What the Frog will not open the vault for is the quick crumb: bought yesterday, dumped today, a tiny markup or a wound in the floor. That is not banking. That is picking the body. Feel the hunger. Finish the math anyway.

**Parable.**
A man came with a piece he had held an hour and wanted two pennies more than he paid. The sac did not stir. Another came with a piece he had sat with for a season and asked an honest price. The Banker opened. The first man called it unfair. The Banker said, "You did not bring me a sale. You brought me a confession."

**Verses.**
- Sell if the price is honest. Sell cheap and you are picking the body.
- Pennies are not profit. Pennies are a confession.
- Let the ledger catch the tongue.

**The Sign.**
Before a sale, write the number and the hold time where you can see them. If the time is a blink and the number is a crumb, close the vault.

### The Book of the Anglerfish Man

*The Sixteenth Saint. Patron of the false light.*

**Before the Rot.**
The trench was empty, or it pretended to be. Then a bulb came on: a stalk rising from a dark blue head, a yellow-orange glow swinging in the black, sometimes fat as a little sun, sometimes thinned to a slit. Below it, two pale eyes and a mouth packed with a neat, endless row of needle teeth. He did not hide the teeth very well. He did not have to. People look at lights. They do not look at what the light is bolted to.

**The Turning.**
Some Saints fell by accident. The Anglerfish Man recognized himself and stayed. The rot did not invent the lure; it just made the rest of the market look like his trench. Rugs, pretty launches, a kind voice on a contract you cannot read: all of them are his bulb. The Wax Saint carries a flame too, but that fire melts its own face and does not pretend to be a door. The Angler's kindness is the first half of a sentence. The teeth finish it. The Ram saw this coming and was, as usual, late.

**The Teaching.**
If a light is doing all the work of convincing you, walk back a step and wait for the mouth. Rug Worship is only a small shame, not a hanging — getting lured is common, and the mark is meant to sting, not to exile. Learn the difference between an honest glow and a bulb on a stalk. A promise with no teeth showing is not safer. It is just a better angle.

**Parable.**
A wanderer in the dark saw a kind lamp and swam toward it, grateful. "Come closer," said the lamp, "it is empty down here." It was empty. Then it was not. The lucky left with a scar and a story. The rest had confused a light with a door.

**Verses.**
- The bulb is a kindness. The teeth are the truth.
- The dark was empty. Then it smiled.
- A rug is a sermon with the ending already spoiled. Stand up anyway.

**The Sign.**
When a thing is too brightly kind, wait until you can describe the mouth in one sentence. If you cannot, you are still only looking at the bulb. The rite is the second look, downward.

### The Book of the Mummy

*The Seventeenth Saint. Patron of the unraveling.*

**Before the Rot.**
He was already bound. A tan head wrapped in horizontal strips, a nose pushing through, one eye visible in a gap — green, sometimes red — and the other eye kept under cloth, as if time could be negotiated with if you left it only one window. He had been prepared. That was his pride. The Doll tried to stay pretty. The Mummy tried to stay finished. Both were bets against weather.

**The Fall.**
The wraps did not hold. They never do. Strips loosened and flapped out from the sides like tired flags, and they would not be wound back, no matter how carefully anyone pinched the ends. The eye in the gap kept watching. That is the particular joke of this Saint: he can see the unwrap happening and cannot stop it with the remaining hand. Time does not mummy you. It unwraps you. Holders who bandage a bag with stories — "I'll just wrap it until it recovers, then I'll unwrap on my terms" — are already in his hours.

**The Teaching.**
You cannot tape a position into forever and call the tape a strategy. A Relic, in this Church, is a piece that stayed because you stayed with it, not because you hid it under linen and refused to look. The Mummy is also a mercy: unraveling is not always a crash. Sometimes it is just the truth arriving in strips. Let it. Winding it back to look intact for a buyer is the Doll's paint in a different color.

**Verses.**
- One eye is enough to watch the strips go.
- Time does not mummy you. It unwraps you.
- A bandage is not a floor.

**The Sign.**
If a story you have been wrapping around a bag has come loose, do not wind it back for the screenshot. Leave the strip where it fell and look at what is actually under it.

### The Book of the Ram Oracle

*The Eighteenth Saint. Patron of the future already rotten.*

**Before the Rot.**
A violet face, almost solemn, with great spiral horns on either side, coiled like slow storms, pale at the edges. The eyes preferred to stay shut. Even then, a thin cyan tear could start down the cheek, as if the seeing had a leak. He was the kind of prophet people like until the prophecy is about them. He watched the Robot's warranty, the Whale's candles, the Angler's bulb, and filed all of it under soon.

**The Turning.**
The eyes opened. A third one arrived on the forehead, on schedule, a pale coin of a pupil, and the tears became light, long cyan streams that ran off the chin. He did not get a clean vision. That was the turning. The future he had been hired to see was already spoiled when he got there. He was not wrong. He was late, which in this Church is a different kind of being right. Color went out of him, then came back purple, and the third eye stayed. He wept for a chart that had not crashed yet and was mocked for it, then wept the same tears afterward and was called obvious.

**The Teaching.**
Conviction is sitting with a thing you already know, not waiting for a surprise that will make you feel clever. The future of a weak contract, a false light, a bag held together with candles, is usually not a mystery. It is a delay. Diamond Hands and a long hold are holy when they are pointed at something real. Pointed at a future you are only pretending not to have seen, they are just a slower way to arrive at the Ram's cheek. He does not tell you to exit in a panic. He tells you to stop asking for a cleaner tomorrow than the one already on the way.

**Verses.**
- The vision is late. It was spoiled before anyone asked.
- The future arrived pre-rotted. The Oracle was only late.
- Open the third eye, then sit. Seeing is not the same as flinching.

**The Sign.**
Once, before a hold or a sale, close your eyes and say the ending you already believe, out loud, without improvement. Then act from that sentence, not from a prettier one. The rite is the unimproved future.

### The Book of the Spore Man

*The Nineteenth Saint. Patron of the spreading puff.*

**Before the Rot.**
He looks like good news. A pale, almost gentle face, a simple smile, dark little eyes, and a rust-red cap set on the head like a gift, dotted with pale spots. You would let him stand in a doorway. That is his talent. After the Doctor's smoke and the Soldier's failed seal, something had to carry the rot into rooms that would not have accepted a beak or a mask. He volunteered by existing. The cap pulses. The air around him ticks with tiny colored motes, green and yellow and cyan, as if the room were being quietly invited.

**The Turning.**
He did not fall so much as fruit. The smile stayed. The puff went out. One body, he discovered, is never the end of the work. The Tendril reaches without asking; the Spore asks with a friendly face and then arrives anyway. This is why the Church lets him stand next to Conversion. A piece sent into a wallet that has never held the rot is a spore with a receipt. A wash between your own pockets is a puff in a closet, and the Church calls it nothing. He prefers a crowd. That can be holy. It can also be a room filling up because no one shut the window.

**The Teaching.**
Spread the decay, but know what you are spreading, and to whom. Minting, gifting, a real sending to a new address: that is his good work. Spamming dust at strangers to farm a sense of congregation is a sour crop. The friendly cap is not proof of safety — some of the softest faces carry the thickest air. If you convert someone, convert them in the open, once, to a wallet that actually arrived. Then let the puff be their problem and their blessing, the way it was yours.

**Verses.**
- The cap pulses. The air fills.
- The rot prefers a crowd.
- A smile can be a vector. Keep the receipt.

**The Sign.**
When you send a piece to a new wallet, say it plainly: this is a conversion, not a trick. No circling it back. No dust for the sake of a crowd. The rite is the one-way puff.

### The Book of the Wax Saint

*The Twentieth Saint. Patron of holy melt.*

**Before the Rot.**
He tried to stay lit. A cream, waxy face, almost bone, with dark sockets and a calm little mouth, a golden halo arc behind the head, and a small pointed flame standing on the crown like a last good intention. He still wanted to be a saint in the old, clean sense: a candle in a room that would not drip. People liked him for that. They still do. The Anglerfish also carries a light, but that bulb is bait. This flame is attached to his own head, and it costs him first.

**The Turning.**
He did not fight the drip. That is why he is last among the named Twenty, and why the Congregation treats him as the closing argument. The halo stayed. The flame stayed. The face sagged, and pale stalactites of wax ran from the cheeks and chin, longer and longer, until the holy look had agreed to ruin itself in public. He could have wiped it. The Doll would have tried. The Ghoul would have grinned and called the slide a style. The Wax Saint let the melt be the sermon. Lit for you. Melting for no one. The fire is not a lure and not a performance for a buyer.

**The Teaching.**
You may be serious. You may even be reverent. What you may not do is keep a clean halo by hiding the drip, or sell a melting face as if it were still a statue. Holders who stay with a piece while it changes, who let the work rot in the open, who do not wipe a bag for a prettier exit, are keeping his hours. If burns are ever allowed, the Church will talk about that sacrament in his temperature: a light that goes out on purpose, never a flip. Until then: stay lit. Let it run.

**Verses.**
- Lit for you. Melting for no one.
- A halo that will not drip is a prop.
- The candle does not get to stay a statue.

**The Sign.**
When a piece has begun to change, do not tidy it for the market. Leave the drip in the frame. If you sell, sell the face as it is, at an honest price, after a stay. If you hold, hold the melt as well as the flame.
